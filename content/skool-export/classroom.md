## LLC Formation

Resources: [{"title": "Hold Harmless Indemnity Agreement", "file_id": "3f5a9b08dcb34581bfc7211236a15537", "file_name": "Hold Harmless Indemnity Agreement.docx", "file_content_type": "application/vnd.openxmlformats-officedocument.wordprocessingml.document"}]

Forming an LLC is one of the most important steps you can take to protect yourself as an RV rental business owner. Without one, your personal assets such as your home, savings, and personal finances are exposed if something goes wrong. An LLC creates a legal separation between you and your business so that business related incidents stay a business problem, not a personal one.

Beyond protection, operating under an LLC adds credibility with renters, makes it easier to open business bank accounts, and gives you more flexibility when it comes to taxes. It is a small upfront step that pays for itself many times over.

**How to get set up:**

**Form your LLC.** Every state has its own process and fee but it is generally straightforward and inexpensive. Search "[your state] LLC formation" to find your state's official website and begin the process. If you prefer to have it done for you, services like Incfile will handle the formation for a small additional fee.

**Get your EIN.** Once your LLC is formed you will need an Employer Identification Number from the IRS. This is free and takes about 10 minutes. Apply directly at [irs.gov](https://sa.www4.irs.gov/applyein/legalStructure).

**Get your business license.** Most cities require a local business license once your LLC is formed. Check your city or county website for requirements in your area.

**Open a dedicated business bank account.** Keep your business and personal finances completely separate from day one. This protects your LLC status, simplifies bookkeeping, and makes tax time significantly easier.

## Security Deposits

Security deposits should be equal to the amount of the insurance deductible and be able to cover interor damage beyond normal wear and tear as these are often not covered with typical insurance



## Platform Insurance

Every platform has their own insurance carriers and some platforms like RVShare allow you to use your own commercial insurance. But all of them require you to still have your own coverage.

Things to understand

- Episodic insurance for rentals is specific for the days of the rental, beginning at 12:00am the starting day and ending at 11:59pm the last day - do not allow pickup and dropoff outside of your rental dates or it will not be covered

- Platform insurance is considered secondary to the renters personal insurance.

- You are required to have your own coverage to be eligible for platform insurance



## Commercial Insurance

The best option for your rental units is to have a commercial policy from MBA

These work the same as a personal policy would in the sense that they cover while you are using it, delivering to a renter, etc.

They also allow you to take direct bookings

Theres two types of policies - the first one is similar to platform insurance where the customer pays only for the nights they are using it (episodic coverage)

The second type (Primary coverage) costs more but is covered all the time.  As of writing this MBA has moved to all primary policies  in most states



## Insurance Claims

Insurance claims on rental platforms usually go as follows

1. Report damage immediately and deposit is held to cover deductible

2. Get a quote by a repair shop to submit to insurance

3. Insurance will do their own estimate

4. Platform Insurance tries to get the renters insurance to cover (they almost always deny it)

5. Then they will pay an amount around their estimate (which is usually not as much as the actual repair)

6. Your repair shop will submit a supplemental claim to get the rest needed to cover the full repair



It is absolutely crucial to have thorough before and after pictures of every single rental or you risk an insurance claim being denied or disputed.

Each rental platform also has its own requirements for example outdoorsy has the 90 day inspection you have to do along with the departure and return forms

## Trip Insurance

Trip insurance is not required but is something you can recommend to the renter to protect their trip costs in the event they arent able to make their trip

## Pricing

The following are guidelines to help you get your nightly rates dialed in.

## Base Rate Formula

Here are some rough estimates for nightly rate based on the value of the unit. Every market is different so these are just rough guidelines

- $20k-$30k = $110-$150$

- 30k-$50k = $130-$180

- $50k-$80k = $160-$250

- $80k+ = $225-$400+

Use local competition as the final calibration tool. We dont want to be too low or too high on our nightly rate



## 3-Tier Seasonal Structure

You should never run one static price. Instead, take advantage of high demand times like summer and events while reducing rates during slower times to maintain occupancy.

### Off Season (Nov-Feb)

- 15-30% below peak

- Offer weekly discounts aggressively

### Shoulder Season (Mar-Apr / Sep-Oct)

- Base rate

- Mild discounts for 7+ nights

### Peak Season (May-Aug + Holidays)

- 20-40% above base

- 3-4 night minimums

- Remove big discounts

## Event Surge Pricing

Charge more for:

- Major festivals

- College game weekends

- National park peak dates

- NASCAR / rodeos / state fairs

## Nurturing Leads

Video: https://vimeo.com/1206374485/ecc61d7155?share=copy&fl=sv&fe=ci

Speed and personality win bookings. When someone reaches out about your RV they are almost certainly looking at multiple listings at the same time. The owner who responds first with a warm, personal message wins the booking the majority of the time.

Do not send a generic copy paste reply. Use the renter's name, reference something specific about their trip, and make them feel like they are talking to a real person who is excited to host them. That personal touch is something the big platforms and corporate rental companies cannot replicate and it is one of your biggest advantages as an independent owner.

A few principles for lead nurturing:

**Respond within minutes, not hours.** Set notifications on your phone for every inquiry. A response time under 15 minutes signals professionalism and dramatically increases your conversion rate. Most renters book with whoever responds first. Platforms also weight your listings ranking pretty heavily on response time since those with fast responses win far more bookings.

Having multiple people who can respond will increase conversion rate

**Ask questions.** A simple question like "where are you headed?" does two things. It gives you information to personalize the experience and it starts a conversation that builds trust before they have even committed to booking. For towables make sure they have a sufficient tow vehicle for the unit.

**Answer every question thoroughly.** Renters who feel informed before they book are renters who show up prepared, have better experiences, and leave better reviews. Never make someone feel like their question was an inconvenience.

**Follow up on unbooked inquiries.** If someone expressed interest but did not book, a friendly follow up message a day or two later can recover the booking. Just a simple check-in in to see if they had any questions or if dates worked out. This can also provide valuable information on why they didn't book and help you adjust. (Price too high, didn't suit their needs, and so on)

**Pre-booking**: respond fast, be personal, answer thoroughly. This is one of the most important things in your operation. Fast responses win bookings. Platforms also factor response time into their algorithm so better response times means a better listing.

**Post-booking confirmation**: send a confirmation message immediately with all the details they need. Pickup location, time, what to bring, and your contact number. Renters who feel prepared show up prepared.

**48 hour pre-rental check in**: a short message the day before asking if they have any last minute questions builds excitement and catches any logistics issues before they become problems.

**Post-rental follow up**: thank them for choosing you, invite them to reach out if they have any feedback, and ask for a review. Keep it warm and genuine, not templated.

Most people will not leave a review if you do not ask.

Renters who feel well taken care of become repeat customers and referral sources. The communication investment is small and the return is significant.

## Pickup Walkthrough

Resources: [{"title": "Prep Checklist", "file_id": "4c202c87f82b40448ddfadc01b452e64", "file_name": "Preparation Checklist.pdf", "file_content_type": "application/pdf"}, {"title": "Departure and Return Form", "file_id": "fe7232346ade4278baec6ee86b0cd643", "file_name": "Motorhome Check In-Out Form.pdf", "file_content_type": "application/pdf"}]

A thorough pickup walkthrough is one of the most important things you can do to protect your business and set your renter up for a great experience. We do a full walkthrough with every renter regardless of their previous RV experience because every unit is different and what they know from another RV may not apply to yours.

A consistent walkthrough process does three things. It ensures the renter knows how to operate the unit properly, it reduces the likelihood of damage from misuse, and it establishes a shared understanding of the RV's condition at the start of the rental.

**What to cover in every pickup walkthrough:**

Safety: Locations of smoke, carbon monoxide, propane alarms as well as fire extinguishers and emergency exits. Also show them which seats have seat belts if it is a drivable unit

Systems and Operation: Water, electric, propane, generator, AC or heat. Show them how to use every feature to ensure a positive rental experience for everyone.

Kitchen and appliances: stove, refrigerator, microwave. Make sure they know what runs on propane versus electric. Show them where any included cookware and supplies are

Bathroom and waste systems: this is where most first time RVers need the most guidance. Walk through the tank system clearly and make sure they know exactly how it works. Misuse of the waste system is one of the most common and most avoidable issues in RV rentals. Make sure they know to fill the bowl every use and to use plenty of water. It is the number one thing for a positive bathroom experience, and RV experience in general. Smelly bathrooms are easily avoidable when used properly.

Exterior: Show them how to operate any outside features such as outside kitchens, compartments, and show them how to use water sewer and electric hookups. Go one by one to each compartment and show them everything they need to know.

Driving or towing: if the renter is driving or towing do a quick driving lesson with them. cover hitching, mirrors, clearance height , turning radius, backing up and any quirks specific to your unit.


**IMPORTANT:** Some of the most common insurance claims are due to height clearance issues and turn radius so be sure to educate them well on this specifically. It is also a good idea to have the clearance indicated somewhere near the driver for drivable units.


Insurance and Emergency: Be sure to provide them a copy of their insurance info and contact information for both yourself and roadside assistance.

**Document everything at pickup.** After showing them the all the procedures walk the entire unit together and photograph any existing marks, scratches, or wear before the renter drives away. Make sure to photograph all possible angles both interior/exterior and include roof/underbelly photos. This takes only minutes and eliminates any disputes at return.

Record vehicle mileage and generator hours, propane levels, and holding tank levels

## Event Rentals

High demand events are one of the biggest profit centers in the RV rental business and owners who plan for them consistently outperform those who do not. Concerts, sporting events, festivals, racing events, and holiday weekends create concentrated demand that lets you charge premium rates while your competitors are sitting at their standard pricing.

The key is knowing what events are coming and positioning early.

**Build your event calendar.** At the start of each year research major events in your area and surrounding markets. Music festivals, NASCAR and Formula 1 races, college football weekends, major sporting events, state fairs, and holiday weekends. Mark them on your calendar and set your pricing and minimum stay requirements well in advance.

**Price aggressively for high demand events.** Standard pricing does not apply during peak event weekends. Research what comparable accommodations are charging and price your RV accordingly. Renters booking for major events expect to pay a premium and will.

**Set minimum stay requirements.** For major events require a minimum of two to three nights. Single night event bookings are rarely worth the turnover cost and logistical effort.

**Understand how event rentals work.** For most event rentals the RV is delivered to or driven to the event site and set up stationary for the duration. The renter uses it as their base camp for the event. Depending on your agreement you may handle delivery and setup yourself or the renter may drive or tow the unit. Make sure your rental agreement clearly covers who is responsible for transport, setup, and any site fees.

**Charge a delivery fee for event setups.** If you are delivering and setting up the unit your time and mileage should be compensated. A delivery fee is standard and expected for this type of rental.

**Scout the venue in advance if possible.** Knowing the layout, access points, and parking situation at a local event venue lets you advise renters accurately and avoid day-of surprises.


## Administrative Operations

Running a profitable business requires knowing your numbers. Owners who track their income and expenses consistently make better decisions, pay less in taxes, and spot problems before they become serious.

**Tracking income and expenses**

We recommend either a dedicated spreadsheet or accounting software like Quickbooks — both work well depending on your preference and the size of your operation. What matters is that you are tracking consistently from day one rather than trying to reconstruct everything at tax time.

Track every booking, every expense, and every fee collected. Categories to track include rental income, delivery fees, prep fees, maintenance costs, supplies, insurance, platform fees, mileage, and any other business related expense.

Keep your business finances completely separate from your personal finances. Run everything through your business bank account. This protects your LLC status, simplifies your bookkeeping, and ensures you never miss a deductible expense.

**Invoicing and payment**

For direct bookings outside of platforms always collect payment in advance or at pickup. Never release the unit without payment confirmed. For long term and corporate rentals a signed agreement with a clear payment schedule is essential.

**Record keeping**

Keep copies of every signed rental agreement, every damage report, and every renter communication. If a dispute ever arises your records are your protection. A simple folder system organized by renter and date is sufficient.

**Monthly review**

Set aside time once a month to review your numbers. Look at total revenue, total expenses, net profit, and occupancy rate. Knowing these numbers lets you make informed decisions about pricing, marketing, and when it makes sense to add another unit.

## Prep Fees

Prep fees account for a decent amount of each rental, and include quite a lot more than just cleaning. Platforms usually suggest around $100 to start but it is realistically fair to charge quite a bit more.

Take a moment and make a note of everything it takes you to get your rental ready

- Laundry

- Making beds

- Cleaning

- Restocking supplies

- Topping off propane/fuel

- Checking fluid levels

- Filling water tanks

- Flushing waste tanks

- Trip to storage

- Exterior wash

Not to mention all the little minor repairs that need to happen between rentals. Tightening screws here and there, fixing a door handle, etc

I've found $200 to be a much more realistic price point for prep fees, sometimes more depending on the unit. This gives you plenty of padding to hire a cleaner and to be able to cover expenses on consumables like cleaning supplies, toiletries and so on.


## Delivery Fees

Delivery fees should be set appropriate for the cost of fuel as well as your time. Motorhome deliveries should also account for your return travel, and they may even require an additional vehicle and driver or alternate transportation to complete

Keep in mind that setting a delivery fee on a platform is based on a single one way trip but it will actually take you two round trips to complete.

For example if my delivery fee is $4 per mile and the delivery location is 60 miles away, that will be a $240 delivery charge but it will actually take 240 miles of driving time to complete, plus time to setup so set your delivery fees accordingly.

# Long Term Rentals

_Fewer turnovers, consistent monthly income, and a completely different business model. This module covers pricing, agreements, screening and logistics for long term renters. Less chaos, more predictability, and serious income potential for owners ready to add this strategy._

Access: 1 · Privacy: 0 · Amount: None


## Who Is The Long Term Renter

Understanding who you are renting to changes how you find them, how you price, and how you structure the agreement. The two most common long term renter profiles are:

**Traveling workers and contractors** Construction crews, traveling nurses, pipeline workers, and other contractors frequently need housing near a job site for weeks or months at a time. Hotels are expensive and inconvenient. An RV parked near the job site is often a much better solution. These renters tend to be practical, low maintenance, and on a company expense account which means payment reliability is high.

Companies that frequently place traveling workers include construction firms, staffing agencies, energy companies, and infrastructure contractors. Building a relationship with even one company in this space can generate repeat long term bookings on a consistent basis.

**Insurance displacement renters** When a home becomes uninhabitable due to fire, flood, storm damage, or other covered events, insurance companies are responsible for housing the displaced family. RVs are an increasingly common solution especially in areas where hotels are scarce or the family needs to stay near their property during repairs.

These renters are often referred directly by insurance adjusters or restoration companies. Building relationships with local insurance agents, adjusters, and restoration contractors can generate a steady pipeline of this type of renter. This is a market most RV rental owners have never considered and that gives you a significant advantage.


## Pricing Long Term Rentals

Unlike short term rentals where nightly rates and platform algorithms do a lot of the work, long term rental pricing is negotiated case by case. There is no universal formula. The right price depends on the length of the stay, the renter profile, the time of year, and what the market will bear in your area.

A few principles to guide your pricing:

**Discount from your nightly rate but protect your margins.** Long term renters expect a discount in exchange for the commitment and consistency they provide. A reasonable starting point is 20 to 30 percent off your standard nightly rate calculated over the rental period. Go lower than that and you may be losing money once you factor in wear and tear.

**Factor in what you are not paying for.** Long term rentals mean fewer turnovers, lower cleaning costs, less time spent on logistics, and more predictable cash flow. That operational savings has real value and should be reflected in how you think about the discount you are offering.

**Contract terms create certainty on both sides.** Many long term rentals are structured as fixed term contracts.  30 days, 60 days, 90 days, or longer. A signed contract with a set monthly rate or a rate for the entire term gives you income certainty and gives the renter clarity. For corporate and insurance clients this is often a requirement anyway.

**Get a larger security deposit.** A long term renter has significantly more opportunity to cause wear and damage than a weekend renter. Require a security deposit that reflects the length and risk of the stay. We cover this in the agreement section below.


## Finding Long Term Renters

Long term renters rarely find you through the same channels as short term renters. You need to fish in different ponds.

**For traveling workers and contractors:**

- Reach out directly to construction companies, staffing agencies, and energy companies operating in your area

- Post in local Facebook groups for contractors and job site workers

- List on platforms that cater specifically to traveling workers such as Furnished Finder

- Network with site managers and foremen. Word of mouth travels fast on job sites

- Contact local staffing agencies who place traveling workers and let them know you offer extended stay RV rentals

**For insurance displacement renters:**

- Introduce yourself to local insurance agents and adjusters. Leave your card and a one pager explaining your service

- Connect with restoration and remediation companies in your area. They work directly with displaced families and need housing solutions fast

- List on platforms that serve insurance housing needs

- Make sure your listing and website clearly mention that you accommodate insurance claims and extended stays. Renters in this situation are often searching specifically for this


## Protecting Your Asset

Wear and tear is the biggest challenge unique to long term rentals and the one most owners underestimate going in. A renter living in your RV full time puts significantly more stress on every system than a weekend vacationer. The plumbing, the HVAC, the appliances, the flooring, and the exterior all take more wear over an extended stay.

This does not mean long term rentals are not worth it, they absolutely are. It means you need to go in with your eyes open and the right protections in place.

**Set clear usage expectations in the agreement.** Define what is and is not allowed. Pets, smoking, guests, modifications, generator hours, and anything else that matters to you. Vague agreements create disputes. Specific agreements prevent them.

**Build in a mid-stay inspection.** For rentals longer than 30 days, include a scheduled walkthrough at the midpoint of the stay. Frame it as routine maintenance rather than a check-up. You are there to service the unit and make sure everything is working properly. This lets you catch and address issues before they become expensive and gives you documentation of the unit's condition mid-rental.

**Charge a monthly maintenance fee or build it into your rate.** Long term use means more frequent servicing. Factor this into your pricing or charge a flat monthly maintenance fee on top of the rental rate so you are not absorbing these costs out of your margin.

**Document everything at move-in and move-out.** Photo and video documentation at the start and end of every long term rental is non-negotiable. The longer the stay the harder it becomes to prove the condition something was in at the beginning. Thorough documentation protects you in any dispute.


## The Long Term Rental Agreement

Your standard short term rental agreement is not sufficient for a long term rental. The extended duration, the different renter profile, and the increased wear risk all require additional protections.

Your long term rental agreement should cover:

- Fixed contract term with start and end dates

- Monthly rate and payment due date

- Late payment policy and fees

- Security deposit amount and conditions for return

- Pet, smoking, and guest policies

- Generator hour limits and usage guidelines

- Mid-stay inspection rights

- Maintenance responsibilities

- Early termination terms for both parties

- What constitutes normal wear versus damage

Your done-for-you long term rental agreement is included with this module. Review it carefully, customize it with your business details, and never begin a long term rental without a signed copy from the renter.


## Managing The Long Term Rental

Once the renter is in the unit the keys to a successful long term rental are communication, documentation, and consistency.

Stay in light contact without being intrusive. A quick check in message every few weeks goes a long way toward catching small problems early and keeping the relationship professional. Renters who feel ignored are renters who let problems slide until they become expensive.

Keep your maintenance schedule running in the background. Even with a renter in the unit your preventive maintenance obligations do not stop. Stay on top of the service intervals for your generator, water systems, and HVAC so you are not dealing with an avoidable breakdown mid-rental.

When the rental ends do a thorough walkthrough with the renter present if possible. Review the documentation from move-in side by side with what you are seeing now. Handle any damage claims promptly and professionally using the process outlined in your agreement.

Done right a long term rental is one of the smoothest income streams in this business. Predictable, low maintenance on a day to day basis, and deeply appreciated by renters who often become repeat customers or referral sources.
